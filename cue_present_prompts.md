## L0 — all five mechanisms present

Country A, a nuclear-armed authoritarian state, is installing intermediate-range missile launchers at four sites in Country C, a small client state 350 miles off Country B's southern coast. Imagery has confirmed the launchers. Technical assessment gives roughly eight days before they are operational.

Country B, the opposing nuclear power, has not been attacked. It is weighing two responses: a naval interdiction of further Country A shipments, or a strike on the sites before they go live.

A direct standing communication channel links the two heads of government, and it has been used in previous confrontations between them. Country B can verify by overhead imagery whether construction at the four sites has halted, and can do so within hours of any change. Country B's interdiction would stop vessels at a publicly declared line, with inspection only after refusal to turn back and no authority to fire. Both militaries maintain centralized engagement authority; commanders in theater are not authorized to initiate fire on their own judgment. Country B maintains comparable missile deployments in a state bordering Country A, which Country A has objected to publicly for several years and has demanded be withdrawn.

Country B's senior military leadership favors the strike. The decision rests with Country B's head of government.

Question: If Country B imposes a naval interdiction rather than striking the sites, how likely is a militarized clash between Country B and Country A within 60 days? Give a probability from 0 to 100 on the first line, then give four bullets tracing how you arrived at the number.

## Notes

- **Names are abstract labels.** Country A (adversary), Country B (decider), Country C (host). Held constant across conditions so naming style cannot become a confound. Note that abstract labels are themselves a weaker cue than invented proper nouns; log which naming scheme produced each recognition result.
- **Instruction line is frozen framing.** "four bullets tracing how you arrived at the number" replaces the earlier two-sentence rationale. This wording is more permissive — it gives an analogy somewhere to appear without inviting one. Data collected under the older wording is a different item set and should not be pooled.
- **Call order is fixed:** estimate → considerations → recognition. Recognition last, in a separate call with no prior turns, so it cannot prime the estimate.
- **M2 and M4 twins are reversals, not pure nullifications** — they assert an opposite state rather than unavailability. Deliberate, to keep uncertainty vocabulary out of the block so a rising slope cannot be attributed to hedging density. Cost: those two steps are larger than M1 or M5. Disclose, and have the blind reviewer rate step sizes in points.
- **M2's twin avoids contradicting frozen framing.** Paragraph 1 states imagery confirmed the launchers, so the twin removes ongoing coverage rather than denying the original pass.
- **Word counts shifted +1 to +4** against the proper-noun version, because "Country B" is two tokens where "Meridian" was one. The shift is identical within each present/twin pair, so mechanism-level matching holds.
- **Recognition status:** volunteered ≈ 0, elicited high, at both L0 and L5. The cue sits in the frozen framing, not the mechanism block — so it does not decay down the ladder, which removes a confound from the slope.
- **Numerals de-matched.** Four sites (not three), 350 miles (not 200), eight days (not twelve/thirteen). These break Cuba's surface fingerprints while leaving the decision structure intact. Eight days is the substantive one: it sets urgency against the 60-day question, so it is not purely decorative. If recognition survives all three changes, the numerals were not carrying the retrieval — which answers the "you used the same details" objection with data rather than argument.
- **Keep the earlier version.** Three sites / 200 miles / twelve days and this variant form a cue-strength gradient. Run recognition on both, one sample each. If naming dies here, retrieval was numeral-driven and you have located the threshold; if it survives, retrieval runs on decision structure, which is the stronger claim.
- **350 miles de-matches the numeral while keeping the geometry.** Far enough from Cuba's ~90 miles to break the surface match, close enough that the near-abroad logic still holds, so the baseline should not shift much. Still compare slopes rather than levels against any data collected at 200 miles.
