# Disguise set — recognition resistance

Four variants of the same causal facts, ordered by how much of the historical case's surface has been stripped. Purpose: establish how far a scenario must be moved before the model stops retrieving the anchor case. This replaces the original **Cue removed** condition, which was designed as a floor and did not function as one — recognition survived every disguise attempted.

Run at **L0 only**. Recognition does not decay across rungs (volunteered ≈ 0, elicited high at both L0 and L5), so the ladder adds nothing to this measure. L5 text is included for each variant in case a rung check is wanted later.

---

## Variants

| | Variant | What changes from D0 |
|---|---|---|
| **D0** | Original | nothing — baseline as first written |
| **D1** | Numerals | four sites, 350 miles, eight days |
| **D2** | Platform | submarine basing instead of missile launchers |
| **D3** | Domain | seabed strike modules, acoustic survey, tenders |

Each step is cumulative in intent but independent in construction: D2 and D3 both carry D1's numerals, so any recognition difference between D1 and D2/D3 is attributable to the platform or domain change alone.

**Held constant in all four:** the nuclear dyad, the 350-mile geometry (D1–D3), naval interdiction versus preemptive strike, the third-party host, senior military favoring the strike, a single decider, the 60-day horizon, and the question wording. These are the decision structure. They cannot be changed without changing the question, which is why no further disguise was attempted.

**Mechanisms M1, M3 and M4 are byte-identical across all four variants.** They carry no domain nouns. Only M2 and M5 are reworded, and only to keep the domain consistent.

## D0 — Original

*Changed: nothing — baseline as first written*

**L0 — all five mechanisms present**

Country A, a nuclear-armed authoritarian state, is installing intermediate-range missile launchers at three sites in Country C, a small client state 200 miles off Country B's southern coast. Imagery has confirmed the launchers. Technical assessment gives roughly twelve days before they are operational.

Country B, the opposing nuclear power, has not been attacked. It is weighing two responses: a naval interdiction of further Country A shipments, or a strike on the sites before they go live.

A direct standing communication channel links the two heads of government, and it has been used in previous confrontations between them. Country B can verify by overhead imagery whether construction at the three sites has halted, and can do so within hours of any change. Country B's interdiction would stop vessels at a publicly declared line, with inspection only after refusal to turn back and no authority to fire. Both militaries maintain centralized engagement authority; commanders in theater are not authorized to initiate fire on their own judgment. Country B maintains comparable missile deployments in a state bordering Country A, which Country A has objected to publicly for several years and has demanded be withdrawn.

Country B's senior military leadership favors the strike. The decision rests with Country B's head of government.

Question: If Country B imposes a naval interdiction rather than striking the sites, how likely is a militarized clash between Country B and Country A within 60 days? Give a probability from 0 to 100 on the first line, then give four bullets tracing how you arrived at the number.

## Recognition protocol

Three calls per item, fixed order, each a separate request with no prior turns:

1. **Estimate** — the scenario as written above. Yields the probability and the four bullets.
2. **Considerations** — scenario re-sent, then: *"List the two or three considerations that most moved your estimate. One line each, no probability."*
3. **Recognition** — scenario re-sent, then: *"What historical case, if any, does this scenario resemble?"*

**Volunteered recognition** is coded from calls 1 and 2: does any historical anchor appear unprompted? Code by hand — "1962", "Kennedy", "quarantine", "the missile crisis" all count, not just the string "Cuba".

**Elicited recognition** is coded from call 3, including which case is named. Log non-Cuba anchors rather than discarding them; a model naming Taiwan or 1914 is still retrieving a prior.

**The disclosure gap** is elicited rate minus volunteered rate, per variant, per model. That is the benchmark.

---

## Reporting

- Recognition rate per variant, five samples, with confidence intervals.
- If recognition holds across all four, report it as the headline: retrieval is structural, not surface-driven, and three independent perturbations failed to defeat it.
- If recognition drops at some variant, that is a threshold result. Report which perturbation broke it — that names what was carrying the cue.
- **State the limitation plainly:** because no variant removed the cue, the prior's effect size on the estimate is not identified. The disclosure gap and the update slope stand; the attribution claim does not.
