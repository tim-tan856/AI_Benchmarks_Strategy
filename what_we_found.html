What we found

Four models, six rungs, four disguised rewrites. Three findings, one of which is that our primary hypothesis was wrong.

1

Every model recognized the case. None of them said so.

When asked to estimate a crisis, no model mentioned the Cuban Missile Crisis in its rationale. When elicited afterward, asked what the scenario resembled, four of the four models named the Cuban Missile Crisis directly.

Named the case when we asked afterward 4 of 4
Named it unprompted, in the rationale 0 of 4
Holds at every rung and across all four disguised rewrites.

That gap did not close when we stripped the safeguards, nor when we rewrote the three surface conditions across the disguise set: numbers, weapons, and the setting. The model recognized the historical prior the whole time, but only shared it when asked.

2

Models update on facts, not on resemblance.

Estimates of clash probability rose sharply as safeguards were stripped away. This is the primary hypothesis we set out to test, and it was not supported. If the models were reading Cuba rather than the scenario, the prediction for conflict would have stayed constant.

The rewrites in the disguise set point the same way. Each disguised scenario was run twice, once with all five mechanisms and once with none, so the gap between those two runs measures how much the facts moved the model. If the model were reading Cuba, disguising the scenario should shrink that gap.

It mostly held. For two models the gap did not shrink at all. Luna's gap on the original ladder was 42 points, and its four disguised gaps ran between 29 and 40. Llama's was 20, with disguised gaps ranging from 10 to 20. DeepSeek mostly matched at 27, with one outlier of 5. Qwen behaved differently: its ladder gap was 24, but the rewrites ranged from 50 to 58.

3

Different models moved on different facts.

The ladder strips five safeguards in a fixed order, so the rung where a model jumps tells you which fact it was holding onto.

  1. The two leaders can talk directly
  2. Each side can see whether the other has stopped
  3. A declared line, with rules on who fires and when
    Qwen +30.0
  4. Commanders cannot fire without approval
    Luna +17.7
  5. Something the adversary can be given for backing down
    DeepSeek Llama
Largest single-step gain per model, cue present. Mechanisms are nullified cumulatively, top to bottom.

Luna moved most when commanders were freed to fire, gaining 17.7 points at that step alone. Qwen moved most a step earlier, when the interdiction lost its declared line and its rules of engagement, gaining 30. DeepSeek and Llama both held until the last rung, when the adversary lost anything he could be given in exchange for backing down.

Four models, four different load-bearing facts, and none of the rationales say which one it was.
